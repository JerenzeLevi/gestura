    Fight guide

    Moves & rules

    Hold a pose clearly for a moment and the AI turns it into a move. Holding it longer does not repeat it — return to Idle, then pose again.

    The symbols below are decoration. The poses the model was trained to recognise are the written descriptions — they are the ones that count. Full recording notes are in model/README.md.

    🧍

    Idle

    Stand relaxed with your hands down.

    Nothing happens. Your stamina recovers.

    ✊

    Jab

    Show a closed fist toward the camera.

    5 damage, 0.5 s cooldown, 5 stamina.

    👍

    Uppercut

    Thumbs up.

    12 damage, 1.2 s cooldown, 12 stamina.

    👎

    Kick

    Thumbs down.

    10 damage, 1 s cooldown, 10 stamina.

    🛡️

    Guard

    Both open palms facing the camera.

    Take only 20% damage while held. Drains 10 stamina per second; at zero your guard breaks and locks for 1.5 s.

    ⬅️

    Dodge Left

    Lean your head to your left, hands down.

    Avoid all damage for 0.5 s. 1.5 s cooldown, 8 stamina.

    ➡️

    Dodge Right

    Lean your head to your right, hands down.

    Avoid all damage for 0.5 s. Shares the dodge cooldown.

    ✌️

    Ultimate

    Peace sign, two fingers up.

    30 damage. Needs a full ultimate meter, which fills by landing hits and by taking damage.

    How a match works

    • Player 1 stands in the left half of the camera, Player 2 in the right half.
    • Each fighter has 100 HP, a stamina bar and an ultimate meter.
    • Rounds last 60 seconds. A K.O., or more HP when time runs out, wins the round.
    • Win 2 rounds to win the match. The match always stops after 3 rounds.
    • Moves need stamina. If you are too tired the move does not happen.
    • A dodge beats everything, including an ultimate.

    How the AI decides

    • Each half of the frame is classified about 7 times a second.
    • Scores are smoothed over time, so one noisy frame cannot fire a move.
    • A move needs 75% confidence, a clear lead over the runner-up, and two readings in a row.
    • A pose fires once. It must be released before the same move can fire again.
    • If nothing in your half reaches 45%, the game treats the zone as empty instead of guessing.

    The project

    About Gestura

    A demonstration of touchless, gesture-based control powered by a custom image-classification model.

    Purpose and real-world use

    Many everyday screens still need touch, a mouse or a controller. Gesture recognition lets people control devices without touching them, which is useful for:

    • Hygiene: public kiosks, hospitals and kitchens.
    • Accessibility: people who cannot use a keyboard or mouse comfortably.
    • Smart TVs and displays: control from across the room.
    • Active play: games that get people moving.

    This app packages that idea as a fun game, so anyone can try touchless control in seconds.

    How the model was trained

    1. Created an Image Project in Google Teachable Machine.
    2. Added one class per move: Idle, Jab, Uppercut, Kick, Guard, Dodge Left, Dodge Right and Ultimate.
    3. Recorded webcam samples of each move from different people, positions and lighting.
    4. Trained the model (transfer learning on MobileNet) and tested it in the preview.
    5. Exported it as a TensorFlow.js model and loaded it in this web app.

    Per-class accuracy is not claimed here. Measure it in Practice with the real model before quoting numbers.

    How it works in the app

    1. The webcam frame is mirrored and split into a left and a right half, one per player.
    2. Each half is resized to 224×224 pixels and classified by the model several times per second.
    3. The scores are smoothed, then checked against a confidence floor and a runner-up margin.
    4. A class that clears both checks twice in a row fires its move, once per pose.
    5. The app checks the model's class names on load and refuses to start a fight if they do not match.

    Technology and privacy

    • Google Teachable Machine (image model, transfer learning on MobileNet)
    • TensorFlow.js and the Teachable Machine image library
    • HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules — no build step and no backend
    • Every frame is classified in the browser. No video, image or prediction is uploaded, and the app stores nothing about you except whether you have seen the guide.

    Getting started

    The camera stays on your device

    Gestura needs your webcam. Your browser will ask once — choose Allow. Every frame is classified locally by TensorFlow.js; no image, video or prediction is uploaded anywhere, and nothing is recorded.

    • The app must be served over http://localhost or https — browsers block the camera for files opened directly.
    • Close Zoom, Teams or the Camera app first; only one app can hold the webcam.
    • Leaving a game screen releases the camera immediately.

    Where each player stands

    Boxing uses one camera for two players. The mirrored frame is cut straight down the middle and each half is classified on its own.

    P1Left half
    P2Right half
    • Stand so your half of the frame is filled from head to waist — that is how the model was trained.
    • Stay inside your half. Crossing the middle puts your pose in the other player's zone.
    • The frame is mirrored, so you move the way you would in a mirror.
    • Each live panel shows a zone check. Both should read “ready” before you start.

    Practice zones

    Practice classifies one zone at a time, and you choose which: P1 (left), Center or P2 (right). The highlighted box shows exactly which pixels are sent to the model.

    • Use the left and right zones to check a move works on both sides before a match — lighting is rarely even.
    • Center is the easiest zone for practising on your own.
    • If the feedback says “move farther into the highlighted zone”, nothing recognisable is in it.

    Lighting and clear poses

    • Light on your face and hands, not behind you. A bright window behind you turns you into a silhouette.
    • Hold the pose still for about half a second. The model sees single frames, not motion.
    • Make hand poses big and keep them around chest or face height.
    • Plain backgrounds and clothing that contrasts with the wall help a lot.
    • Return to Idle between moves — a held pose fires only once.

    What the confidence numbers mean

    The model gives every move a score out of 100%, and the bars under each game show them live. A move fires only when:

    • the top score is at least 75%,
    • it beats the runner-up by a clear margin, and
    • it stays on top for two readings in a row.

    If nothing reaches 45%, the zone counts as empty and no move fires. Those rules are shared by both game modes, so a pose that scores well in Practice will behave the same in a match.
